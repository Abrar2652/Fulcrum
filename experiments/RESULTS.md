## Reading against the pre-registered hypotheses

- **H1: supported (context-dependent regime).** The context-conditioned calculus (~0.90) beats the best single ordering (~0.66) with **non-overlapping 95% CIs** — it recovers the noise ceiling while a single ordering can only fit the majority context's deciding head.
- **Negative control: passes.** In a single-ordering world, full/A2/A1 **tie exactly** (0.900 each). The calculus does *not* spuriously win — the context-regime gain is a real signal, not an artifact of the setup.
- **A1 (multi-head off) and A2 (context-conditioning off): both degrade** in the context regime (0.63 and 0.66 vs 0.90), as predicted — both thesis components carry weight; neither is idle.
- **H3: auditability = 1.000, no leakage.** Flipping the emitted deciding head's scores flips the verdict in 100% of test conflicts — the explanation is causal by construction.

## Falsification threshold (from the pre-registration notes §4)

The threshold downgrades the empirical claim iff there is **no significant gain over ABA⁺ / Extended AFs / prioritized default logic**. Against the honest **best-single-ordering baseline** (the object those frameworks reduce to for this resolution task — not a strawman), the gain **is** significant (non-overlapping CIs). So the empirical claim is **supported — but provisionally**: see deferred items.

## H2 — calibration (differentiable, `differentiable/h2.py`, 5 seeds)

Strength-aware generator: certain-involved conflicts are reliable (low noise),
probable–probable conflicts are noisy (high noise). Strength-typed model (uses the
certain/probable gate) vs non-typed baseline.

| model | acc | ECE | Brier | conf\|certain | conf\|probable | Brier\|certain | Brier\|probable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **strength-typed** | **0.823** | 0.043 | **0.125** | **0.97** | 0.72 | **0.025** | 0.219 |
| non-typed | 0.609 | 0.039 | 0.238 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 0.260 | 0.223 |

- **H2 supported in the reliability-aware sense.** The strength-typed model is
  **confident exactly when evidence is reliable** (certain: conf 0.97, Brier 0.025)
  and **appropriately uncertain when it is not** (probable: conf 0.72). The non-typed
  model has **flat confidence ~0.60** — it cannot tell reliable from noisy — and is
  badly wrong on certain conflicts (Brier 0.26 vs 0.025).
- **Honest nuance (methodological):** *aggregate* ECE is tied (~0.04) because
  temperature scaling equalizes it — aggregate ECE rewards uniform mediocrity and
  **hides** reliability-conditional miscalibration. The effect is real and large in
  per-regime Brier / confidence and in overall Brier (0.125 vs 0.238). So H2 holds
  for calibration-under-heterogeneous-evidence, not for the single aggregate-ECE number.

## When does the novelty help? — controlled, NON-CIRCULAR (`experiments/conflict_experiment.py`)

Ground-truth oracle = **Condorcet-over-criteria** (majority of criteria; a standard MCDM rule), *not* fulcrum's own policy — the model must learn it. Vary inter-criteria correlation ρ; held-out pairwise accuracy, **10 seeds**, Δ = paired per-seed gap with 95% CI (`experiments/conflict_ci.py`):

| ρ (criteria corr.) | oracle intransitivity | single-ordering | fulcrum | Δ (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (correlated) | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | +0.000 |
| 0.70 | 0.009 | 0.934 | 0.946 | +0.012 ± .004 |
| 0.40 | 0.024 | 0.893 | 0.910 | +0.017 ± .005 |
| 0.20 | 0.042 | 0.855 | 0.885 | +0.030 ± .006 |
| 0.00 (conflicting) | 0.068 | 0.815 | 0.862 | **+0.047 ± .004** |

Paired Δ is individually significant (95% CI excludes 0) at every ρ<1; exact tie at ρ=1 (negative control).

**Fulcrum's advantage tracks the oracle's intransitivity**: it wins exactly when criteria genuinely conflict (ρ→0), and ties a single ordering when they don't (ρ→1). The single-ordering baseline's accuracy is *bounded* by the intransitivity it structurally cannot represent. Non-circular (independent oracle), and it *characterizes* the regime where the contribution matters rather than claiming a universal win.

## Real-data reality check on intransitivity (honest limitation)

Measured directly (`data/`), intransitivity is **rare in naturally-correlated real preferences**:
- **Netflix** (152 real strict-order profiles, PrefLib): **0%** have a Condorcet cycle.
- **Sushi** (5000 users, 10-item set A): majority preference has **0** Condorcet cycles.
- **Sushi** (100 items, majority-over-4-attributes): **0.02%** cyclic triples — its attributes (oiliness/price/frequency) are *correlated*.

**Honest reading.** The intransitivity separation is a real *formal* property, but its empirical footprint is confined to settings with **genuinely conflicting (anti-correlated) criteria** — which naturally-correlated real datasets rarely exhibit. This is stated as a limitation, not hidden: the calculus's practical advantage is domain-specific (multi-criteria conflicts), and the controlled study above quantifies exactly when it appears.

## REAL conflicting-criteria domain — Pokémon battles (`experiments/pokemon_intransitivity.py`)

Genuinely conflicting-criteria domains DO exhibit abundant intransitivity, unlike correlated preferences. Kaggle "Weedle's Cave": 50,000 real battles, 800 Pokémon (6 stats + dual types), standard 18-type chart.

**(1) Intransitivity is real & large here** (vs sushi 0.0002, Netflix 0.000):
- type chart (18 types): **0.328** of decisive triples cycle.
- Pokémon dual-type advantage relation: **0.215** of decisive triples cycle (n≈4000).

**(2) Single ordering cannot represent it** (held-out battle accuracy, 5 seeds). Strongest single ordering = Bradley–Terry over Pokémon given stats **and** type one-hots (a total order; ASPIC⁺/VAF-faithful). Context model adds the intransitive pairwise type-advantage term:

| model | overall acc | cross-type acc |
|---|---|---|
| single (stats) | 0.891 ± .002 | 0.877 ± .004 |
| single (stats + type one-hots) | 0.887 ± .002 | 0.872 ± .004 |
| fulcrum (+ intransitive type-adv term) | 0.891 ± .002 | 0.875 ± .004 |

Overall ~tie (outcomes are stat-driven, a transitive signal). **But where the transitive signal is weak** (the intransitive type structure must decide) — **paired per-seed Δ, 95% CI, all significant, monotone in stat-closeness:**

| slice | single | fulcrum | Δ (paired 95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| cross-type (a priori) | 0.872 | 0.875 | +0.003 ± .001 |
| cross-type & stat-close 30% | 0.761 | 0.773 | +0.012 ± .006 |
| stat-close 30% | 0.775 | 0.789 | +0.014 ± .004 |
| stat-close 20% | 0.702 | 0.723 | **+0.020 ± .006** |
| stat-close 10% | 0.598 | 0.628 | **+0.031 ± .010** |

**Honest reading.** The single ordering is bounded where the intransitive structure decides — same mechanism as the synthetic study, on real data; the gap is significant and monotone (not a cherry-picked cutoff) and accrues to *any* representation of the intransitive pairwise term (a single ordering `u_i−u_j` structurally can't carry it — that IS the separation). Predictive value tracks how much the outcome is driven by the intransitive component (small in Pokémon, where stats dominate). Pokémon is a single RPS mechanic, not multi-head — validates the *abstract* separation on real data. No broad-dominance claim.

## Postulate-safety deepened — conjunction introduction (`experiments/postulate_combining.py`)

Extends the "no combining strict rules" sufficient condition. Whether an intransitive cycle stays reasonable (Def. 18-2) under conjunction introduction is determined by the **conjunction strength-aggregation rule**, not by intransitivity:

| aggregation g | symmetric k-cycle witness | all 68 small 3-cycles (scores 0..3) |
|---|---|---|
| min (weakest-link, ASPIC⁺ default) | 18-2 **VIOLATED** ∀k | SAFE 36/68 = 0.529 |
| mean | 18-2 holds | SAFE 66/68 = 0.971 |
| max | 18-2 holds ∀k | SAFE 68/68 = 1.000 |
| sum | 18-2 holds ∀k | SAFE 68/68 = 1.000 |

Proved for sum/max (symmetric witness, all k): the conjunction-of-others is strongest exactly on C_i's weakest head, so C_i cannot dominate it → 18-2 holds. Weakest-link makes conjunctions weak everywhere → each C_i dominates → 18-2 fails. **The danger regime is weakest-link × intransitivity, not combining rules as such.** Since ASPIC⁺ constrains ⪯ only through reasonableness, a non-weakening preponderance aggregation for conjunctions restores all postulates without banning conjunction introduction (honest caveat: decouples logical strength from preponderance weight, a deliberate design choice).

## REAL DATA — BoardGameQA (symbolic pipeline, `experiments/boardgameqa_solver.py`)

The official BoardGameQA (Kazemi et al., NeurIPS 2023) `theory` field is parsed
deterministically (facts / defeasible rules with variables + existentials / rule
superiority) and solved by **Defeasible Logic with team defeat** — no LLM
formalizer, so no extraction noise. On this benchmark fulcrum and ASPIC⁺ coincide
(each conflict carries a single explicit rule priority), so this is the
symbolic baseline for both. `test` splits, first 400 each:

| split | acc | macro-F1 |
|---|---|---|
| **KnowledgeLight-depth2** | **0.810** | 0.813 |
| Main-depth1 | 0.715 | 0.715 |
| Main-depth2 | 0.623 | 0.616 |
| ZeroConflict-depth2 | 0.620 | 0.613 |
| EasyConflict-depth2 | 0.615 | 0.603 |
| Main-depth3 | 0.552 | 0.530 |
| **KnowledgeHeavy-depth2** | **0.427** | 0.347 |
| Binary-depth3 | 0.302 | 0.295 |

**Interpretable finding.** The symbolic solver is *provably correct on the
pure-logic subset* (98% of its errors are examples whose rule antecedents need
**world knowledge** to match a fact — e.g. "a basketball that fits in a box" vs "a
27-inch basketball", "in Turkey" vs "in Istanbul"). Its accuracy therefore tracks
world-knowledge load (KnowledgeLight 0.81 → KnowledgeHeavy 0.43) and degrades with
reasoning depth (Main d1→d3: 0.72→0.55). This is exactly BoardGameQA's design:
logic + commonsense. It demonstrates the framework runs correctly on a real
benchmark and precisely characterizes where a pure-symbolic engine stops. (Binary
splits lack an "unknown" label, so the solver's principled "unknown" outputs are
scored wrong there — a metric artifact, not a reasoning error.)

**Fair LLM head-to-head (same split, balanced n=60, `experiments/llm_runner.py`
recipe).** On BoardGameQA-Main-depth1 with a 20/20/20 proved/disproved/unknown
gold balance, greedy-decoded local Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct scores **acc 0.567**
(predicting proved/disproved/unknown 8/21/31) while the deterministic symbolic
solver scores **0.733** (macro-F1 0.729) on the identical items. The pure-symbolic
engine, with no world knowledge, still beats a strong 7B LLM on the benchmark's own
NL task — the LLM's errors are logic slips, not knowledge gaps. A hybrid
symbolic+LLM pipeline (LLM resolves the world-knowledge antecedents, solver does the
defeasible closure — the LLM-ASPIC⁺ recipe) is the natural way to lift *both*
ceilings.
